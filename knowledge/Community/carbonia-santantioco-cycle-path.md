The cycle path connecting Carbonia to Sant'Antioco, passing through San Giovanni Suergiu, runs on the bed of a narrow-gauge railway that Ferrovie Meridionali Sarde built between 1923 and 1926 to link Sant'Antioco and the lower Sulcis to Cagliari. The Sulcis railway line was shut down in 1974.

> **At a glance:** The line was still running through the 1970s, well into living memory for anyone who grew up here in that decade — it isn't some distant 19th-century relic, but part of ordinary local transport within a lifetime of today's older residents.

## From Railway to Cycle Path

After the 1974 closure, the Sulcis railway sat disused for decades. Conversion into a cycle path began around 2014, and the Carbonia-Sant'Antioco stretch was substantially completed after roughly four years of work, with the province conducting final inspections around mid-2020 — though as of that inspection some sections still required maintenance work before the route could be considered fully finished. A related branch continues from Giba, following the former Siliqua-San Giovanni Suergiu-Calasetta line, also dismantled in the late 1970s and likewise partly converted to a cycle route.

![The converted cycle path near Is Gannaus and Funtanona, on the former rail bed](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/32/Pista_ciclabile_ex_FMS_Carbonia-San_Giovanni_Suergiu.jpg)

## What the Route Covers

The path follows the old rail corridor from Carbonia through San Giovanni Suergiu toward Sant'Antioco, tying the town to one of its most-visited neighboring destinations by a route that requires no car. It sits alongside — rather than replaces — the ARST bus service that also runs the Carbonia-Sant'Antioco corridor in roughly half an hour.

![The cycle path near San Giovanni Suergiu's industrial area, on the former rail bed](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6d/Pista_ciclabile_ex_FMS_Carbonia-San_Giovanni_Suergiu_2.jpg)

## Practical Information

The route runs at grade on the former rail bed between Carbonia and Sant'Antioco via San Giovanni Suergiu. As with any repurposed rail-trail, surface conditions vary by stretch and maintenance has been an ongoing, not one-time, project since the 2020 completion milestone.
