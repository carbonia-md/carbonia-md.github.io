Monte Sirai is a Phoenician-then-Punic settlement on a plateau 191 meters above sea level, about 2 kilometers northwest of Carbonia. Excavations began in 1963, after a local resident found a carved stele at the tofet the year before; the site has drawn visitors ever since, though it wasn't formally inaugurated as an archaeological park — with today's visitor center, ticketing, and parking — until 2001. The site is unusual for Sardinia in a specific way: it carries no later-period construction or restructuring layered on top, so what's there is Phoenician and Punic, without a Roman or medieval overlay confusing the picture.

> **At a glance:** The site's tofet held cremation urns for children and animals, placed at the base of an elevated cult building reached by a staircase — a specific, documented detail rather than a vague "ancient religious site" gloss.

## A Site Without Later Layers

The plateau was first settled by Phoenicians and then restructured by Carthaginians during the Punic period, drawn by its strategic position between sea and land, controlling a road axis and access to mineral deposits. Because no later civilization built over it, the settlement, necropolis, and tofet at Monte Sirai preserve a continuous Phoenician-to-Punic sequence that's rarer on the island than a multi-period site would be.

## The Necropolises and the Tofet

The site comprises three sectors: the settlement itself, the necropolises, and the tofet. The Phoenician necropolis holds cremation burials in small graves cut into tuff. The Punic-period hypogeal necropolis, northwest of the tofet, comprises 13 underground family tombs, their interior spaces marked by sarcophagi, pillars, and burial niches; a separate children's necropolis uses amphorae as burial containers, and a more recently discovered burial area consists of single pit graves. The tofet itself dates to the 4th-2nd century BC: an elevated location reached by a staircase held a small cult building, while cremation urns containing the remains of children or animals were deposited at its base.

## The View

Monte Sirai's plateau looks out over the Gulf of Palmas, with the islands of Sant'Antioco and San Pietro visible on a clear day, along with smaller surrounding islets.

![The Sulcis archipelago seen from Monte Sirai, with Sant'Antioco and San Pietro visible](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a1/Arcipelago_Sulcis_da_Monte_Sirai.jpg)

## Practical Information

The park is roughly 2 kilometers northwest of central Carbonia. Opening hours and admission are managed by the Comune di Carbonia's museum and archaeological-park system; check current times before visiting, as they vary by season.
