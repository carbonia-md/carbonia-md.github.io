Piazza Roma is the central square of Carbonia, a town founded in 1938 as a rationalist-architecture showpiece for Mussolini's Fascist government. In 2007, a study by five European universities ranked it among the 60 most livable squares in Europe[^1].

> **At a glance:** The square's civic tower, the Torre Littoria, was explicitly built as a symbol of political power — and the square it dominates now also holds the last work sculptor Gio Pomodoro ever made, unveiled after his death.

## The Church of San Ponziano

Built in 1938 alongside the square, the Chiesa di San Ponziano reinterprets medieval church forms in a modern, rationalist key: a rectangular granite façade with a small porticoed atrium, its upper section faced in red trachyte. The bell tower has a square base and stands over 44 meters tall, modeled at reduced scale on the campanile of Aquileia. At ground level, it houses a votive chapel dedicated to Santa Barbara, the patron saint of miners — a direct link between the church and the coal-mining town built around it.

## The Torre Littoria

Beside San Ponziano stands the Torre Littoria, the town's civic tower: five floors, 27.5 meters tall. Visitors describe it plainly as a Fascist-era political symbol, and its position next to the church and the municipal building follows the same logic that shaped the whole square — public power made monumental and centralized.

## Frammento di Vuoto I

On 1 May 2005, following the square's early-2000s restoration, Piazza Roma gained a sculpture: Gio Pomodoro's *Frammento di Vuoto I*, carved from an 80-ton block of Carrara marble with a volume of roughly 40 cubic meters[^2]. It was Pomodoro's last work: he died in his Milan studio on 21 December 2002, so the piece was completed and installed posthumously. The sculpture is built around the relationship between fullness and emptiness, manipulating the marble to define its opposite (the void), and has been described as evoking a sail.

## Practical Information

Piazza Roma sits at the center of Carbonia, alongside the Torre Littoria and the Chiesa di San Ponziano. The square is pedestrian and open at all hours; the church's visiting hours follow normal parish schedules.
